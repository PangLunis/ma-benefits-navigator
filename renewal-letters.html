Got a renewal letter? How to keep your benefits

Checked against MassHealth, DTA and Social Security on November 17, 2026.

The short version

  • Send it back by the date on the letter, even if nothing has changed. If it doesn't come back, benefits can stop even when you still qualify.
  • Use the form that came with your letter. If the letter has an e-Submission number, you can do the whole thing online instead.
  • A line like “your assets are too high” does not end the help paying your Medicare premium. That program has no savings limit.
  • Before you mail it, go through the checklist below and take a photo of every page.

MassHealth or Medicare Savings renewal (65 and older)

Once a year MassHealth checks that you still qualify. When it can't do that on its own, it mails a letter and a form, often the Renewal Application for Certain Seniors Living in the Community. This also renews the Medicare Savings Program (the help paying your Part B premium) and the Health Safety Net.

Ways to send it back:

  • Online: if your letter has an e-Submission number, fill it out and upload it at mhesubmission.ehs.mass.gov/esb. Nothing to mail.
  • Mail every page to the address printed on your form. On the current form that's MassHealth Enrollment Center, PO Box 4405, Taunton, MA 02780-0968.
  • Fax every page to (857) 323-8300.
  • In person at a MassHealth Enrollment Center, for example 529 Main Street, Suite 1M, Charlestown. Walk-in times are limited, so MassHealth suggests booking an appointment.

The page-1 box marked SNAP means “send this form to DTA as a new application for food stamps.” If you already get SNAP, leave it unchecked.

“Your assets are too high”: what it does and doesn't mean

  • The Medicare Savings Program has had no savings limit since March 2024. Savings alone can't end your Medicare premium help, as long as the form goes back and your income is under the limit.
  • Full MassHealth coverage (the MassHealth card used at the doctor and pharmacy) still has a $2,000 savings limit for one person. That's the part savings can affect.
  • Send the form back anyway. Leaving it unanswered is the one sure way to lose everything.
  • Don't move or give away money to get under the limit without asking first. A free SHINE counselor can explain how it's counted.

Before you mail it: a checklist

  1. Sign it, and date it the day you mail it. Not the date you started filling it in.
  2. Print your name on the line under your signature, if there is one.
  3. List every bank account with your name on it, including joint accounts. If a family member manages your Social Security as your representative payee, list that account too: Social Security's rules say the money in it belongs to you.
  4. Other health insurance: write in your Medicare Advantage or other plan, with the member ID from the card.
  5. The SNAP box: check it only if you want to apply for food stamps.
  6. Send all the pages of the form, not just the ones you wrote on.
  7. Take a photo of every page before it goes in the envelope, and write down the day you mailed it.
  8. Haven't heard back in about a month? Call MassHealth at (800) 841-2900 (Mon–Fri, 8–5) and ask if they got it.

If you missed the date

Send it in anyway, and call MassHealth at (800) 841-2900. MassHealth hearing decisions describe a window of about 90 days after coverage ends to return the renewal and be reinstated without starting a new application. After that, you'll likely need to apply again.

If MassHealth cuts or ends your coverage

  • You can ask for a fair hearing (an appeal) using the form that comes with the notice. It must reach the Board of Hearings within 60 days of when you got the notice.
  • To keep your benefits while you appeal, get the request in within 10 days of getting the notice, or before the benefits stop, whichever is later.
  • Free help: SHINE at (800) 243-4636, or a legal aid office through masslegalhelp.org.

Food stamps (SNAP) renewal

  • DTA mails a recertification form before your SNAP period ends. Fill it out on DTA Connect or return the paper form by the date on the letter.
  • If everyone in your home is 60 or older (or disabled) and no one works, you usually only renew every 3 years, with no reports in between.
  • Questions: DTA Assistance Line, (877) 382-2363.
